Understanding Japan's Two Numbering Systems

Japan uses two distinct numbering systems for 1 to 10, plus dedicated counter suffixes for people, objects, and order items. Understanding when to use which is essential for shopping and dining!

1. Sino-Japanese System (漢数字)

Used for: Prices, phone numbers, age, general counting, numbers 11-100+

1 - 10
1 (Ichi) 一 (いち)
2 (Ni) 二 (に)
3 (San) 三 (さん)
4 (Shi/Yon) 四 (よん)
5 (Go) 五 (ご)
6 (Roku) 六 (ろく)
7 (Nana/Shichi) 七 (なな)
8 (Hachi) 八 (はち)
9 (Kyuu/Ku) 九 (きゅう)
10 (Juu) 十 (じゅう)

2. Native Japanese System (和語)

Used for: Ordering items (1-10 items at restaurant/store) without specific counters!

1 - 10
1 item (Hitotsu) ひとつ
2 items (Futatsu) ふたつ
3 items (Mitsu) みっつ
4 items (Yottsu) よっつ
5 items (Itsutsu) いつつ
6 items (Muttsu) むっつ
7 items (Nanatsu) ななつ
8 items (Yattsu) やっつ
9 items (Kokonotsu) ここのつ
10 items (Tō) とお

People Counter System (人 - Nin / Hitori / Futari)

Crucial for restaurant entry & booking! Notice 1 & 2 people have irregular native pronunciations.

1 - 5 People
1 Person 1人 (ひとり) Hitori
2 People 2人 (ふたり) Futari
3 People 3人 (さんにん) Sannin
4 People 4人 (よんにん) Yonnin
5 People 5人 (ごにん) Gonin
Konbini & Restaurant Greeting Tip: When staff welcome you, they will ask "Nan-mei sama desu ka?" / "Gomeisama desu ka?" (How many guests / Party of 5?). You reply: "Gonin desu" (5人です).
